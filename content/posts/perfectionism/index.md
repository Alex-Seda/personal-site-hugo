---
title: "Perfection: The Barrier to Starting"
date: 2026-09-22
tags: ['reflection']
summary: '"When we wait until we are ready, we will be waiting for the rest of our lives." - Lemony Snicket'
---

"What is the best opening line?" I think to myself as I stare at the cursor blinking on my screen.

Another blank document.

Nothing is written yet.

Everything I am going to write is still a concept, an idea.

Until I formulate it into words, it is just that: an idea. Until I write it down, it cannot be judged. Until it is expressed, it cannot be bad or wrong.

---

For a long time, I have wanted to write more.
I have wanted to have a public place to share my thoughts and reflections with others.
Maybe my posts won't get read, and maybe posting them is pointless.
But at least they won't be kept to myself.
They would have the possibility of being of use to someone else.

However, every time I have sat down to write, I have found myself drafting, rereading, and being unsatisfied with the state of my work.
What began as a thought that I wanted to share somehow became the first draft of a published work that would be studied by others.

Or so it felt.

All of my drafts began to pile up. One by one, they became a rough draft for a post that I planned to publish at a later time.
Because, obviously, my draft was not yet ready to be posted.
Certainly, there was much more editing to be done before they could be posted.

For some reason, I felt that everything I wrote would be scrutinized.
Judged for worth.
And I felt that it had to measure up.

It needed to sound intelligent.

It needed to be insightful.

It needed to impress others.

Somewhere along the way, I forgot why I sat down to write in the first place.
Not as an effort to draft a philosophical essay, but simply to share something I had been thinking about.

And this is where I feel many of my ideas and aspirations often end up.
I want so badly to ensure that I am ready when I present my work.
I want to make sure it is right.
So, I wait until I feel that my work is proofread enough, or that I have obtained enough skill, or that I feel ready to present what I have created.

The catch is that often that time never comes.

I wait until I feel that my writing sounds good enough, but I always see imperfections, so the drafts pile up.
I wait until I feel that I am talented enough at guitar before playing in front of other people, that I know enough songs, but I always feel like a beginner, so I continue to practice alone.
I wait until I feel that my programming skills are good enough before offering my skills for anything real, but I always see how much I have to learn, so I never move past projects.

Then I saw a video of someone describing the same exact feeling.

They said that they got out of it by just creating anyway.
Painting a picture and it turning out imperfect, but it was yours.
Trying to cook a dish and burning it, but you still tried.

And this got me thinking about the times that I have been forced to present something that I believed was imperfect:

- When I was asked to give a lesson at Church and I felt it was imperfect, but afterward I got comments about appreciating the insight.
- When I had a deadline to deploy a website that I felt could be better, but the team told me that it turned out great.

The more I thought about it, my belief that my work was not good enough did not mean that it had no value.
In each situation, delaying until I felt ready would have meant I waited forever.
And in each situation, someone benefited from the fact that I presented or deployed it anyway.

Of course there were things that could have been improved.
But the deadlines forced me to act, and it always turned out fine.

And that is really the whole point of these thoughts: when it comes to creating and pursuing goals, something is always better than nothing.
And waiting to feel ready results in nothing.

My desire for perfection was preventing me from enjoying what I was doing, and it was preventing others from benefiting from what I had to offer.

Maybe this post could sound better if I spent more time on it before posting.

But maybe that's not the point.
