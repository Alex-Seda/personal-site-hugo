The Flood. Sodom and Gomorrah. The Ten Plagues. Nadab and Abihu. The Conquest of Canaan. Uzzah and the Ark of the Covenant.

The Old Testament has many examples of God executing judgement for sin. We see stories of individuals killed on the spot and commands to wipe out nations.

When we turn to the New Testament, however, we seen the lame healed and the blind regain sight. Stories of redemption and healing, teachings of love and forgiveness, patience and tenderness.

Was the God of the Old Testament a mean, cruel God? Was Jesus unlike His Father? Or did God change His mind and become nicer?

I would argue that the God we see in the Old Testament and Jesus, that we see in the New Testament, are the same, in nature and character.

## Understanding Scripture

The way we view Scripture changes our understanding of it.

Knowing how to view Scripture can be difficult, though. What other book was written over the span of 4000 years, by around 40 different authors, spanning multiple literary genres (historical narrative, law, poetry, wisdom literature, prophecy, and letters)?

We are accustomed to viewing Scripture as primarily instructive material in religious settings. We attend worship service on Sunday morning and expect a lesson to be given from Scripture. We read Scripture to discern how God wants us to live.

Maybe we read to hear about His love for us. To hear about how He forgives us. To take comfort in His plan for our life.

While these are completely legitimate reasons to read and study scripture, I believe that they are not the complete picture. After all, why should I care about reading the Old Law if we do not follow it anymore? What purpose do the Major Prophets serve in giving me application to my life? Why would I care to read the repetitive prophecies of the Minor Prophets? How am I benefited by reading the personal greetings of Paul to the Christians he was writing to?

_"For whatever was written in former days was written for our instruction, that through endurance and through the encouragement of the Scriptures we might have hope." (Romans 15:4, ESV)_

Written for our instruction in what?

I would submit for your consideration that Scripture has always been written so that we may know God. That is it.

From the beginning, God has created us for relationship with Him. Since the Fall in Genesis 3... (lost close relationship, he makes a way back)

Now, the purpose does not free us from the literary genre, but it does change how we understand it... (the law was meant to be read as law, but the purpose of the law was to bring Israel closer to God and that they would represenbt God to the nations, etc.)
